influence of UAS-lidar flight parameters on tree detection at N1 study site

George Woolsey

22 June, 2026

1 Overview

This analysis seeks to evaluate the influence of UAS-lidar flight parameters on forest inventory accuracy

1.1 Objective

The primary objective of this research is to determine how variations in UAS-lidar flight configurations of altitude and speed influence the accuracy of individual tree detection, tree-level measurements (height and DBH), and stand-level structural quantification.

1.2 Data & Field Validation

We collected UAS-lidar data and stem-mapped field inventory data within a 9.3 ha management unit located in the Manitou Experimental Forest in Colorado, USA. The field-collected reference data will serve as the ground-truth for all structural measurements, physical attribute dimensions, and stand-level aggregations. The overstory at the site is predominantly ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa), characterized by a distinct cohort of younger trees infilling canopy gaps left by a historical 1980s bark beetle outbreak, alongside a minor component of Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii). Current management objectives of the stand and surrounding forest are focused on reducing hazardous fuel loads and restoring historical, heterogeneous spatial patterns to mitigate wildfire risk.

1.3 Point Cloud Processing via cloud2trees

Raw spatial point clouds were processed, filtered, and segmented using the cloud2trees R package. This framework automates point cloud processing for individual tree forest inventories with tree-level measurements including location, DBH, CBH, and more.

1.4 Accuracy Evaluation

  • Detection Accuracy: tree-level instance segmentation matching pairing UAS-detected trees with stem-mapped field trees. The resulting True Positive, False Positive, and False Negative counts are used to calculate precision, recall, and F-score.
  • Quantification Accuracy: tree-level accuracy of physical attributes of tree height and DBH for the successfully matched tree pairs. We use RMSE to capture typical error magnitude, MAPE to express relative precision as a percentage, and ME to quantify overestimation or underestimation of tree form.
  • Stand-Level Accuracy: assessment of stand-level measurement accuracy that compares aggregated forest inventory metrics derived from the entire field inventory against all UAS-predicted trees. We calculate absolute bias and relative percent differences for stand basal area and trees per hectare across the different flight configurations to evaluate total structural summary performance.
